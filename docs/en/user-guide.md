# User guide

Lotse is an AI assistant for your online shop (Shopware 6, Shopify, Magento 2, OpenMage). You write what should happen in the shop. Lotse asks follow-up questions, shows you a preview or a comparison first, and writes **only after your approval**.

> The AI has read-only access. Writing is done exclusively by the server, after you click “Apply”, and every change can be undone.

## 1. The interface

| Area | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Workspace** | Chat, context and preview. Start page with shop check and examples |
| **Style guide** | Brand (colors, fonts, tone) and custom rules, in one dialog |
| **Knowledge** | Documents (data sheets, PDFs) as a source of facts |
| **History** | All chats; a dot shows whether a turn is running, waiting for you or finished |
| **Settings** | Connect shop, connect AI, image AI, AI access, logs, language |

New chat: **New chat** or **New chat with context** (choose the target right away). Chats stay in the history and can be reopened, including draft and approvals.

## 2. A chat in brief

1. **Write what should happen.** Examples: “Change the layout of the product page of product XY”, “Lower all prices in the Jackets category by 20%”, “Add an FAQ to all products”.
2. **Answer follow-up questions.** Lotse asks targeted questions (clickable answers) when something is unclear: scope, tone, order. It does not invent missing facts.
3. **Review the preview or comparison.** Pages appear on the right as a draft, data changes as an old→new list.
4. **Approve.** Only “Apply” writes to the shop.
5. **Undo if needed.** “Undo” restores the previous state.

You can attach files (images, CSV, Excel, PDF, Word, text; by clicking, pasting or dragging). Lotse uses them as a source, e.g. photos for new products or a supplier list for an import.

### Context
Use the target icon to choose a category, product or landing page. The chat then refers to it, and the real page appears on the right. Without a selection, Lotse works out the target from your message. Choosing a different context in a chat that already has a history automatically starts a new chat, so histories stay cleanly separated.

## 3. Preview

The preview on the right has two views:

- **Current**: the real page in the shop.
- **Draft**: Lotse's draft, in your shop's theme.

**Draft on the real page (Shopware).** Header, footer, menu, buy box, description/reviews, cross-selling, product listing and filters are the real parts of the target page. Only the content in between is replaced by your draft. If you don't name a product, Lotse picks a real active product with an image (for category layouts, its category). 

**Draft in the real theme (Shopify, Magento, OpenMage).** The preview shows the shop's real target page (product, category or collection; for landing pages the home page as the frame) with header, footer and theme CSS. Inside it is exactly the HTML that publishing writes: above and below the theme content (product list, product area, position of the `theme-main` block), for Magento and OpenMage products in the product description, for landing pages in place of the page content. If the page cannot be reached (e.g. a password-protected shop without a content area), Lotse shows the simple preview with the style guide colors.

**JavaScript in the preview.** The shop's menu, sliders and gallery work, but isolated: the preview has no access to Lotse, cookies or your session, and third-party scripts (tracking, consent services) are not executed. Features that need requests to the shop (e.g. the cart flyout) do not work in the preview.

**Annotate.** With **Annotate** you click elements in the preview and note what should be different. The notes (“requests”) are collected and sent together as one task with **Send … requests to Lotse**. This works in both “Current” and “Draft”.

**Mobile view.** The phone icon switches to a narrow layout.

**SEO/CRO review.** For the draft, Lotse shows a rating with concrete points (headings, links, images, structure). One click opens the list.

## 4. Approval and undo

Every change to the shop goes through a **change set**:

- For each row you see **before → after** with warnings (e.g. price −99%, price below purchase price).
- You can **deselect rows**; only what is selected is applied.
- “Apply” writes to the shop at a throttled rate (at most 300 entries per set; bulk rules beyond that, see below).
- Every application is recorded in the **Changes** log (Settings → Logs), with time, scope and result.
- **Undo** restores the old values. For layouts, each page is reset individually and the new layout is removed again.

**Writing is atomic where it makes sense:** if a step of a layout publication fails, everything is rolled back and nothing half-done remains in the shop.

**Bulk.** Rules for many products (“lower all prices by 20% and round to .99”) are calculated by the server, not the AI. You see groups, a sample and errors instead of thousands of rows.

## 5. Layouts and pages

Lotse builds Shopping Experience layouts (Shopware) or page content from blocks: text, image-text, sliders, FAQ, custom HTML sections and more. The draft matches your **style guide** and reuses existing building blocks of your shop.

### Where should a layout apply? (Shopware)
Before a layout for categories or products is published, Lotse asks, with real numbers, **where** it should apply:

| Target | Selection |
|---|---|
| Category | one category · several selected · all categories |
| Product | one product · several selected · all products of a category (incl. subcategories) · all products in the shop |
| Landing page | own page with URL and sales channel |

The overview shows how many pages **lose their own layout** as a result (“30 products, 4 of them with their own layout, will be replaced”). At most 1000 pages per publication; narrow the selection first for more. Meta title and description only apply to a single category; for several, add them afterwards via chat per category.

## 6. Style guide and custom rules

The **style guide** describes how your brand looks and sounds: colors, fonts, radius, tone (informal/formal address, dos and don'ts), typical building blocks. Lotse derives it from theme, CSS, logo and real copy (“Analyze style guide”) and applies it to everything.

- **Colors** can be adjusted and saved directly in the dialog.
- **Everything else** you change in the chat (“Switch to informal address”).
- **Detected building blocks** are collapsed; they are material for the AI.

### Custom rules
Fixed requirements for all copy, for example:

- “SEO texts on product pages have at least 2000 characters.”
- “We never say ‘cheap’, we say ‘fairly priced’.”
- “Dimensions always in cm.”

Each rule has a **scope** (everywhere, product copy, pages, SEO) and optionally a **category** to which it applies exclusively (then also to its subcategories and products). You create rules in the style guide dialog or in the chat (“Remember: we address our customers informally”); Lotse asks before saving. They apply in every chat in addition to the style guide and take precedence in case of conflict. At most 100 rules of 300 characters each.

## 7. Knowledge

On the **Knowledge** page you store documents: data sheets, PDFs, Word, Excel, CSV, text (up to 25 MB, no customer or order data). Lotse stores them locally and uses them as a **source of facts** for product copy, FAQs and safety information (e.g. GPSR).

- Documents can be linked to products and categories.
- Every statement taken from the knowledge base names its **source** (document and page).
- If a fact is missing, it is left out or asked for, never invented.
- Excerpts are sent to the AI provider (Anthropic or OpenAI) as part of the prompt.

## 8. Shop check and custom checks

On the start page, the **shop check** shows what is currently wrong in the shop: SEO, images, data, prices, links, languages, legal and accessibility (technical check, not legal advice) and Search Console. Each item has a severity (Important, Medium, Note), a count, examples and **Add as task**: this puts a ready-made task into the input field; it is only sent when you click.

The check runs in the background (stale after 6 hours) or via **Check again**, and automatically after changes have been applied.

### Managing checks
**Checks** opens the list of all checks:

- **On/off** per check. A disabled check does not run and does not appear.
- **Custom checks** (marked “custom”) can be deleted.
- **Create new check in chat**.

### Custom check via chat
Examples:

- “Warn me when product descriptions are shorter than 2000 characters.”
- “Report products with fewer than 5 items in stock.”
- “Check whether the meta description is missing and there is no image.”

Lotse turns this into conditions on fixed fields and shows you **the rule in words and the number of matches in your shop before saving**. Only after “Yes” is it created, and it then appears as a regular finding.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Words / characters of the description | Length without HTML |
| Characters of the meta title / meta description | 0 = missing |
| Characters of the product name | |
| Price, list price | gross |
| Stock | where the platform provides it |
| Has an image | yes / no |

Conditions can be combined with **and** (all) or **or** (at least one); comparisons: <, ≤, >, ≥, =, ≠. Unknown values never match; nothing is guessed. Custom checks see active products, not categories, pages or links. At most 20 custom checks with 5 conditions each.

### Impact of changes (Search Console)
If Search Console is connected, Lotse remembers the search data of the previous 28 days when you apply a change: for meta title and description, name, description, short description, FAQ, title and content of CMS pages, and for new layouts or page content of existing categories and products (at most 50 pages per change set, only pages Google already knows). Under **SEO → Impact of your changes** you see the state of each change: *Waiting for data* (with the date from which it can be measured), *Preliminary* (from 14 days) or *Measured* (after 28 days), with clicks per day, click-through rate and position compared, and a verdict (*Improved*, *Got worse*, *No clear effect*, *Too little data*). **Measure now** asks Search Console about all open changes without writing anything. If a change did harm, **Open chat** takes you to the change set, where you can undo it; the shop check reports such changes too. Measurements can be hidden.

### Sales: top sellers and slow movers
If the **Sales** switch under Settings → AI access is on, Lotse reads only **totals per product** from the orders of a period: quantity, revenue (line totals) and number of orders, without cancelled orders. Neither Lotse nor the AI sees customers, addresses or single orders. You can then ask: “What are my top sellers of the last 30 days?”, “Which products don't sell?” or “Which top sellers have weak content?”. In the **Abverkauf** (sales) area the shop check reports top sellers with a thin description, missing metas or no image, and slow movers (stock but no sale in 90 days). Shop requirements: Shopware integration with read access to orders; Shopify app with `read_orders` (without `read_all_orders` only 60 days); Magento integration with access to Sales → Orders; OpenMage with the `Lotse_Connector` module 1.3.0 or later.

| **Connect AI** | Provider (Claude via API key or Codex via ChatGPT subscription), model, **monthly budget** (cost brake for Claude: once reached, no new run starts; each run uses at most half of the free budget, also with parallel chats; from 80 % a notice in the chat; below it the costs of this month and today) |

| **AI access** | Which areas Lotse may read (products, categories, layouts, media, marketing, shop basics). Blocked areas are not read, not even by the shop check. Separate switches (off by default): **Sales** (only totals per product from orders, see below) and **Internet** (web search) |
| **Logs** | **Access** (every read access by the AI, rejected ones marked) and **Changes** (approved, rejected, undone, changed) |
| **Search Console** (optional) | Access for opportunities, content gaps and impact measurement |
| **GitHub** (optional, under Integrations) | Fine-grained token for code changes to plugins and themes. On GitHub under *Settings → Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens*, select only the repositories you need and set **Contents** and **Pull requests** to *Read and write* (for Lotse to create projects for new plugins itself: also **Administration** to *Read and write* and “All repositories”, see [Without a developer](#without-a-developer-build-your-own-plugin)). The token is stored encrypted, tested automatically (shows the GitHub login and repositories without write access) and never shown again, not even to the AI. Lotse only writes to its own `lotse/…` branches, never to `main` |
| **Sandbox** (optional) | Switches on **Sandbox Coding** (off by default): code tasks in their own Shopware containers (Dockware) on this machine, plus memory, CPUs and the idle pause (stopped, not deleted). Needs Docker access, see [Self-hosting](self-hosting.md#enable-sandbox-coding). Set the image, environment variables, own setup commands and **Notes for the AI** in the **Sandbox Coding** tab; secret values are encrypted and never shown; code in the container (including code the AI writes) can read them, so only enter credentials meant for the sandbox alone |
| **Language** | German or English for the interface and AI replies (see below) |

Customer, order, payment and credential data are permanently blocked and cannot be unlocked.

### Sandbox Coding: a sandbox in the chat

A sandbox is a **development environment connected to Git, in which Claude Code or Codex work**: a working copy of your repository (without a project: a local git) and a test shop of its own (Shopware in Docker), where the AI makes changes, checks them and saves its steps as commits – nothing is published until you click, as a pull request or as a plugin ZIP to upload to your shop. It works with a plugin or theme, a new plugin, a whole Shopware project, an own setup or no project at all. You attach a sandbox to an ordinary chat of the workspace: **Start sandbox** (with a `</>` icon next to the context below the input) opens the choice – your [saved environments](#saved-environments-set-up-once-a-fresh-sandbox-for-every-job), **Demo shop without a project** and **Set up new …**, the “Start sandbox” dialog in three short steps – **What?**, **Code source**, **Data** (see [Start sandbox: every case](#start-sandbox-every-case)). If Docker is not reachable, the button is locked and the dialog says so with a link to the guide. The AI can also propose a sandbox itself (“Proposal: start a sandbox” with its reason and the same choices, a fitting saved environment or its project preselected) – it is only created when you click **Start sandbox**; the reason becomes the sandbox’s title (**Set up new …** opens the same dialog). Afterwards the card shrinks to one line. There is at most one sandbox per chat. Lotse sets up a Shopware container of its own in the background; next to the chat, instead of the draft preview, the sandbox appears. Its header has two lines: title and status, below them repository and working branch (with a copy button; the full name is in the tooltip). On the right are the internet state, the one fitting action (**Stop**, **Pause** or **Resume**) and the **⋯** menu with **Replace database**, **All sandboxes** and **Finish sandbox**; while an action runs, the status reads “Pausing …” or “Starting …” (and no button contradicts it; “Resume” only appears once the container has really stopped). Below are the tabs **Preview**, **Setup**, **Changes**, **Review** (the pull request) and **Console** (numbers are neutral; a coral dot means something needs you, e.g. a failed step or new feedback). During the setup **Setup** is open: at the top “Step 5 of 12 · about 2 min” with a progress bar, below it every step with status and duration; the log opens by itself for the running and the failed step, for the others on click. If a step fails, one line below it states the cause (the full output is in the log), **Retry from here** continues there, for your own commands **Edit the command in the sandbox environment →** leads straight to the sandbox environment, and the chat reports the failure too. Once the sandbox is ready the view switches to **Preview** and the setup folds into “Set up in 2:45 min ✓” (**Show details** opens it again). You can already write during the setup: the AI starts once the sandbox is ready. **Pause**/**Resume** stops and starts the container (code, database and changes stay; after longer inactivity Lotse pauses it by itself). **Finish sandbox** (in the **⋯** menu) deletes container and files – if feedback on the pull request is still open, the confirmation warns about it; the sandbox is detached, the chat stays and is a normal chat again (marked “Sandbox (finished)” in the history), the sandbox stays as an entry in the **Sandbox Coding** tab. That tab is an overview: readiness at the top (one line when everything is set up, otherwise a note on what is missing), then all sandboxes with status, repository and PR link with its state (“PR #1 · open”, “merged”, “closed”; a click opens its chat, **New sandbox** starts a new one in a new chat – with the same choices), then the **Saved environments**, the stored databases and the collapsible **Sandbox environment**. Memory, CPUs and the idle pause are under Settings → Sandbox; both pages link to each other.

**With your own database:** in the dialog’s **Data** step, choose **Demo data** (default: Shopware’s sample shop from the Dockware image), a **Stored database** or **Upload a dump**: a **database dump** (`.sql` or `.sql.gz` from `mysqldump`/`mariadb-dump`, without `--databases`/`--all-databases`) by drag & drop or file picker – only sanitized dumps without customer and order data. The upload runs in chunks with progress; **Cancel** pauses it, **Resume** continues where it stopped. **Store for further sandboxes** is ticked by default: the dump then joins the stored databases and stays until you delete it there. Without the tick it is a one-off dump and is **deleted right after the import** (also if it fails); the timeline shows “Dump deleted ✓”. The setup first checks whether the dump matches the image’s Shopware version: if the major version differs (e.g. dump from 6.6, image 6.7) it stops with a hint – better choose a matching image in the sandbox environment, or import anyway with **Continue anyway**. Then Lotse imports the dump, points the domain to the sandbox, installs your plugin, runs the own commands “after the database” and compiles the theme. **Replace database** in the **⋯** menu imports another stored database or a new dump later; your code stays unchanged.

**Stored databases:** the **Sandbox Coding** tab lists them with name, detected Shopware version, size, when and by whom they were uploaded and when they were last used. **Upload database** stores a new one, **Delete** (with confirmation) removes the file and its snapshots; sandboxes that already imported it keep their database. Under **Environment → Presets** you preselect one as **Database** for “Start sandbox”. **Faster start with a snapshot:** the first sandbox with a stored database imports it and then saves a copy of the finished database (step “Save a database snapshot”, the list then shows “Snapshot ready”). Every further sandbox with the same database and the same image takes over that copy before it starts, without an import; the timeline shows “taken over from the snapshot”. Each sandbox works on its own copy – what the AI changes in one is not seen by the others. After the image changes, the next sandbox imports again and saves a new copy.

The **Preview** shows the sandbox storefront – for trying it out like a real shop: navigation, search, cart, checkout and customer account work (Lotse keeps the session per sandbox on the server). It fills the tab edge to edge. At the top you enter a path (e.g. `/checkout/cart`), reload, switch between desktop and mobile or open the preview **in a new tab**. If the space is narrower than Shopware’s desktop width (992 px), “Desktop” shows a scaled-down 1280 px view, so the storefront really shows its desktop layout; below about 700 px the preview starts in mobile width (the desktop view would hardly be readable there) – “Desktop” still switches. More room: drag the border between chat and preview (or use the arrow keys) – Lotse remembers the width in this browser –, **Collapse chat** or **Full screen** (Esc ends it). **Admin** opens the sandbox’s Shopware administration (best effort). Symfony’s debug toolbar and Shopware’s cookie notice are switched off in the sandbox (step **Tidy the preview**), so they cover neither the preview nor screenshots. After the theme is compiled the preview reloads by itself. If the sandbox is paused, **Resume** (or **Load preview**) starts it again; until the storefront delivers its first page (Shopware recompiles its templates after the start, which Lotse does before “Ready”) it shows “Environment starting …” – at normal size, not scaled down. The preview counts as activity. If starting a paused sandbox fails, the preview does not keep retrying on its own (not even in a browser tab of its own): it shows the error with **Start again**, and only that click starts the container once more.

**Console:** in the **Console** tab, **Open console** opens a shell of your own in the sandbox’s container – like a terminal on the test shop, for developers and the curious: `bin/console`, logs in `var/log`, Composer, `mysql`. It runs as user `dockware` in the shop directory `/var/www/html` (with an own setup the code is in the target folder, e.g. `cd /opt/app`), files belong to `www-data` as usual, and `sudo` works as usual in Dockware. The buttons **Clear cache**, **Compile theme**, **List plugins** and **View log** type the matching command into the console. Up to three consoles at once (**+ New console**); the size follows the area, copy and paste work with Ctrl+Shift+C/V (Mac: ⌘C/⌘V). If you reload the page, an open console reconnects and shows its recent output. If the sandbox is paused, **Open console** starts it again; input counts as activity. Without input Lotse closes a console after 30 minutes, and also on pause, finish, a new setup and when Sandbox Coding is switched off – the console then says why. Closing ends everything you started in the console, also commands with `sudo` and background jobs; only services you restart (e.g. `sudo service mysql restart`) keep running. The AI has no access to the console; it can tell you commands to run there yourself. The audit log records who opened and closed a console when, not what was typed.

**Take over from the container:** what comes into being in the console or through an AI command inside the container – `bin/console plugin:create`, `composer require` in a plugin, a generated migration, build output that belongs in the project – exists only in the container at first. It reaches the project (and with it commits, the pull request and the ZIP) only when you take it over: **Changes** tab → **Take over from the container** → **Compare with container**. Lotse compares only the folders it transfers into the container (the plugin folder; for a whole project `custom/plugins`, `custom/static-plugins`, `custom/apps`, `config/packages`, `config/services*.yaml` and `src/`; for an own setup the target folder) and lists new, changed and deleted files with a diff. What the fresh container already had right after its setup (files of the image, of your setup commands) does not count as a change – nor does a project file the setup changed or removed, as long as nobody touched it since. A file only counts as deleted when Lotse could see its folder in the container completely: below a symlink, an unreadable folder or a file in place of a folder it is not offered (the folder is listed under “Not compared”). The reasons are shown in your language. `vendor/`, `node_modules/`, `var/` and `public/` are skipped unless the project already has files there (e.g. a built administration in `src/Resources/public`). Tick the files (or **Select all**) and **Take over files** – exactly the version you saw is taken over; if it changed in the container meanwhile, Lotse refuses it (“compare again”). The same checks as for a commit apply: `.env*`, keys and `.git` are never taken over, GitHub workflows, files over 5 MB and anything that looks like a secret cannot be selected (with the reason); symlinks, device files and unusual file names are listed under “Not compared”. Files taken over are then normal changes not saved yet: the AI saves them with its next step, otherwise they are added when submitting. The AI can do the same itself (tool `pull_from_container`, optionally only certain files or folders; in a sandbox set up before this feature only with named files or folders) – every file taken over then appears as a card in the chat. Every take-over is in the audit log. As long as you take nothing over, the file stays in the container: Lotse transfers only what changed in the project since the last transfer (also outside the AI’s tools), and the whole folder only when it does not know what the container has (e.g. after a new setup).

**Working on the code with the AI:** in a chat with a sandbox the AI works like a developer – and **only** in the sandbox: the live shop is not reachable from there (no shop tools, no change sets). It reads and edits the repository’s files with its own file tools, runs commands inside the container (e.g. `bin/console theme:compile`, logs, the sandbox database; with an own setup in the target folder) and checks the page itself. Every changed file appears as a card with “+/−” lines, every command with its result (e.g. “Compile theme ✓ 7 s” or the error); after compiling, the preview reloads. Like Claude Code, the AI saves every finished, checked step as a **commit** with its own message (tool “Save change”) – locally in the sandbox only: no push, no branch switch, no rewriting of earlier commits; the author is “Lotse”, with you as co-author. It does not commit protected files, GitHub workflows or anything that looks like a secret (the same check as publishing, without “anyway”). The **Changes** tab shows **Take over from the container** at the top (see above), then the **saved steps** (commits, newest first, each “local” or “published”) and – if any – what is **not saved yet**, below that all changes against the base branch: a collapsible section per file with “new”/“deleted”, +/− counts and line numbers; a click on a file in the list jumps there, and it refreshes by itself after every file change. If the sandbox is paused, your message starts it again (“Environment starting …”) – the chat waits until Shopware answers. Requests marked with **Mark** in the preview go straight to the AI. The AI can change files only in the sandbox’s repository; `.git`, `.env` and keys are blocked for it, commands run only inside the container (no internet, never on the Lotse machine), Lotse tells it environment variables by name only (commands in the container can read them, though; output is redacted – only enter credentials there that are meant for the sandbox alone), and it publishes nothing itself: at the end it proposes title and description for the pull request. Claude turns count towards the cost brake; with Codex the chat runs on the ChatGPT subscription.

**Building plugins:** the sandbox is not only for themes. In a plugin repository the AI also changes PHP classes, `services.xml`, settings (`config.xml`), migrations, storefront templates and administration extensions; it knows the structure of a Shopware plugin, makes changes effective in the container itself (`bin/console plugin:refresh`, `plugin:install --activate <Name>`, `plugin:update`, `database:migrate`, `theme:compile` and `cache:clear` last – in the reverse order a newly added template of a freshly installed plugin stayed invisible in our test) and checks them (preview, `plugin:list`, logs, its own console commands or calls to the test shop’s Store API). **A new plugin from scratch:** choose **New plugin** in the dialog and an empty repository (or create one with **Create a new project**) – at most `README`, `LICENSE` and `.gitignore`, as GitHub creates it (at least one commit, otherwise there is no base branch). The dialog notices (“The repository is still empty …”) and asks for the **technical name of the plugin** – its class and folder name in Shopware, e.g. `CoNineHeaderBanner` (letters and digits, starts with an upper-case letter; a name derived from the repository name is suggested). When the AI proposes the sandbox it can pass the name along (shown on the card); without one for an empty repository, **Start sandbox** opens the dialog. The setup reports “The repository is empty – new plugin …” at **Detect plugin**, skips **Install and activate plugin** and – without a database dump – also **Compile theme** (“will be skipped (new plugin)” in the timeline) and is ready afterwards; with a dump, the imported shop’s themes are compiled and the cache cleared as usual. The AI then creates `composer.json` (type `shopware-platform-plugin`, `extra.shopware-plugin-class`, `autoload`) and the plugin class, installs and activates the plugin in the container and then builds the actual feature; the files end up in the sandbox under `custom/plugins/<Name>`. As soon as `composer.json` and the class exist, Lotse treats the repository like any other plugin – a new setup (e.g. **Retry from here** at **Detect plugin**) then installs it normally. The pull request contains all new files. **JavaScript and administration:** storefront JavaScript and administration extensions have to be built (`bin/build-storefront.sh` or `bin/build-administration.sh` in the container). Both scripts ship with the Dockware image but download npm packages on their first run: offline the storefront build stops at once (`EAI_AGAIN`), the administration build hangs. So the AI asks for internet for it (see below); with internet the first build takes several minutes (not verified in Lotse’s test environment, whose containers have no internet). Twig, SCSS (via `theme:compile`), PHP and configuration need no build.

**Style guide and your own rules apply here too:** if a style guide is stored in Lotse, the AI gets it as data in the sandbox chat – translated into code: colours, radius and fonts via SCSS variables (in a theme the Shopware theme variables such as `$sw-color-brand-primary` or the fields in `theme.json`, in a plugin its own variables defaulting to those) instead of hard-coded colour values, visible texts as snippets for German and English in the tone and form of address of the style guide. Your own rules for “Everywhere” and “Pages” apply to these texts as well (rules for product texts, SEO or single categories do not). Without a style guide the AI says so once and follows the shop’s existing theme variables. Both are local Lotse data – the sandbox does not access the live shop for them.

**Internet for the sandbox (only after approval):** the sandbox container is offline. If the AI needs internet (e.g. for `composer require` or `npm install`), it asks: the chat shows “Allow internet access for the sandbox?” with its reason; only your click on **Allow for this sandbox** connects the container to the internet (**Decline**: it continues without). Such requests have a coral border because they are a risk decision; if you block internet again later, the card says “Allowed – blocked again since”. You can also switch it yourself in the sandbox header: **Internet: off** allows it, with **Internet: on** (yellow badge) **Block** blocks it again. Access applies to this sandbox only and ends when you block it, pause (also automatically after inactivity) or finish; after “Resume” the container is offline again. **With internet the container reaches any public address – including your live shop’s website** like any other site. So only allow it when needed. Lotse itself stays unreachable from the container. In the environment (**Sandbox Coding** tab), **Internet in the container** is “only after approval” (default) or “never”. Approvals and revocations are in the audit log with the user.

**Submit for review (pull request):** the interface speaks plainly – “Save change” means commit, “Submit for review” pull request, “Project” repository; the technical term is shown small next to it or as a tooltip. Once the change is checked in the preview, the AI proposes title and description (“Proposal: submit for review”); the description is shown formatted (lists, bold, code). **View and submit** opens the **Review** tab with that proposal; once the pull request exists, the card says “Submitted as pull request #42” with a link. The tab shows the pull request at the top (once there is one), then the form with the changed files and the route `lotse/<title>-<id> → <base branch>`, adjust title and description and click **Submit for review** (the button stays visible at the bottom) – only this click publishes. Progress runs as the steps Save change (commit) → Upload (push) → Submit for review (pull request); afterwards the tab scrolls to the top and reports “Submitted for review: pull request #42 created.” Lotse pushes the AI’s commits as they are and only turns what is not saved yet into one last commit (title and description as the message; author “Lotse”, you as co-author when you are logged in with `LOTSE_PASSWORD`) – all to the task’s own working branch and opens the pull request against the base branch (not a draft). Lotse sets branch and target, not the AI. Changes to `.github/workflows` and `.github/actions` (and `action.yml` anywhere), protected files (`.env*`, keys, `auth.json`) and anything that looks like a secret (tokens, private keys, URLs with a password, credentials stored in Lotse) are not published: Lotse stops before the push and names file and line. Stored credentials only count when they are fit to be a secret – a plain shop password like “shopware” does not block every `composer.json` with `shopware/core`. For a remaining false alarm (a stored value or an example URL with a password) you publish after checking with **Publish anyway**; the approval is logged with file, line and your name, never with the value. Keys and tokens cannot be approved. Afterwards chat and tab show the card “#42 … – open” with its state (open, merged, closed), the checks on GitHub and **Open on GitHub**; the task list shows the number. Further changes go into the same pull request with **Update submission**; the button only appears when something changed since the last push (or you edit title or description), otherwise it says “Everything is submitted”. A sandbox without a project first shows **Create as GitHub project** here instead (see [Start sandbox: every case](#start-sandbox-every-case)). Below, the tab offers **Download plugin as ZIP** (see [Without a developer](#without-a-developer-build-your-own-plugin)). If something stops it, the reason is shown in one line in the tab, with the full report from git or GitHub collapsible below: missing write access of the token (only on a real 403 from GitHub), branch protection for `lotse/*`, a branch changed on GitHub, another refusal by the server (“Push rejected: …”, e.g. a pre-receive hook or a file that is too large), the GitHub rate limit or a Lotse restart; clicking again continues there without creating anything twice. Nothing is published while the AI is working (and vice versa).

**Feedback on the pull request:** while the pull request is open, Lotse asks GitHub for news about every two minutes (only with Sandbox Coding on and GitHub connected). Reviews, comments, line comments and failed checks appear as a card **Feedback on pull request #42** in the sandbox’s chat – each piece of feedback only once. The texts come from third parties; the AI only sees them when you click **Hand over to AI**. That starts a new turn: the AI gets the feedback as marked data (redacted and capped), works through it in the sandbox – a paused container is woken first – and then proposes a new description; publishing again takes your click on **Update submission**, into the same pull request. When the pull request is merged or closed, a card says so with **Finish sandbox**, and Lotse stops asking. Once the sandbox is finished, feedback can no longer be handed over: for further changes start a new sandbox (a stored database can be picked there again; a one-off dump has to be uploaded again).

**Screenshots (optional, by hand only):** neither the setup nor publishing captures anything by itself. If you want images for the pull request, switch on the **Screenshot button in the preview** in the sandbox environment (**Sandbox Coding** tab; default: off) and set the **Pages for screenshots** (one path per line, at most 5, default `/`). Then **Screenshot** (camera icon in the preview bar, in the menu when the panel is narrow) captures these pages on desktop and mobile – for example once before and once after the change. The **Review** tab shows the captures only when there are some (newest first, at most the last six; a click opens an image in full size). The images stay in Lotse (they are deleted with **Finish sandbox** and on a new clone); the pull request only mentions them in one sentence, without a link and without an upload to GitHub. The capture runs in a short-lived container with a fixed browser image (`mcr.microsoft.com/playwright`, about 2 GB once; “Check environment” pulls it while the button is switched on, otherwise the first click does); it is attached only to the internal network and reaches only its sandbox’s storefront. If a capture fails (page not answering, sandbox paused), the preview says why.

### Whole Shopware project

Instead of a single plugin you can also choose the repository of your **whole shop** – laid out like `shopware/production`, with several plugins under `custom/plugins/` (and `custom/static-plugins/`), apps under `custom/apps/`, its own configuration in `config/packages/` and project code in `src/`. Lotse recognises it by its `composer.json` (type `project`, requires `shopware/core`); the setup then shows **Detect Shopware project** and **Bring in the project**.

- **Shop in a subfolder:** If the Shopware project is not in the root but in e.g. `shop/` or `apps/shop/` (with other things next to it, such as a frontend, infrastructure or a README), Lotse finds it itself (up to two levels deep, even when another `composer.json` sits in the root); the start dialog says “Detected: whole Shopware shop in the folder shop/”. Only that folder goes into the test shop; everything next to it stays outside, but the AI can change it and it is published too (never `vendor/`, `node_modules/`, workflows or secrets). Paths in the repository carry the folder in front (`shop/custom/plugins/<Name>`), in the test shop the same files are at `/var/www/html/custom/plugins/<Name>`; the AI knows both. If several Shopware projects are in the repository, `"projectDir": "shop"` in `.lotse/sandbox.json` (see below) says which one is meant.
- **How the project gets into the test shop (completely, the default):** Lotse transfers the **whole repository** to `/var/www/html` and installs it as on a server: `composer install` from your `composer.lock`, database migrations, then plugins (also those your `composer.json` requires as a package, e.g. `frosh/tools`), apps, theme. `composer install` needs internet in the container – the setup then **waits for your click** on **Internet: off** at the top of the sandbox (and confirm) (status “Waiting for internet”, also in the chat). Right after `composer install` the container is offline again. **Private packages** (e.g. from `packages.shopware.com` or private Git repositories) need credentials: add a **secret** environment variable `COMPOSER_AUTH` with the content of your `auth.json` in the sandbox environment. **Never transferred:** `.env` files, keys and credentials, `vendor/`, `var/`, `files/`, the generated folders below `public/` (`bundles`, `theme`, `media`, `thumbnail`, `sitemap`) and `config/jwt`.
- **“Overlay” mode (without internet):** If the sandbox environment says “Internet in the container: never”, or `.lotse/sandbox.json` says `"projectInstall": "overlay"`, the test shop’s Shopware stays as it is. Lotse lays the plugins, apps, the configuration (`config/packages/`, `config/services*.yaml`) and `src/` over it – each file at the same path as in the repository. Then all plugins of the project are installed and activated (static plugins via composer, as in production), apps installed, and the project’s theme assigned to the storefront and compiled. **Never transferred:** `.env` files, keys, `composer.json`/`composer.lock`, `config/bundles.php`, `vendor/`, `var/` and `public/`.
- **Services the test shop does not have:** Production configuration often points at Redis, OpenSearch, S3 or a CDN, a mail server, RabbitMQ, Sentry or external logging. Lotse switches that to local **in the test shop only** (files, MySQL, mail catcher, search via MySQL …) – with a file of its own that never goes into your repository. What was switched and which configuration files are not loaded in the test shop at all (e.g. for packages it lacks, or PHP/XML configuration below `config/packages/`, which Lotse cannot check – write configuration as YAML) is shown in the **Setup** tab in the box **Whole Shopware project** and in the step’s log. If Shopware still does not start, the step shows Shopware’s message and the likely culprit file.
- **With a database dump:** The database then decides which plugins are active and which theme is assigned; only plugins it does not know yet are installed. If the repository has a plugin in a newer version than the database (e.g. a branch not yet live), Lotse updates it with `plugin:update` including its migrations. Active plugins whose code is neither in the project nor in the test shop (e.g. from the store) are named in the log – they are missing in the test shop.
- **Shopware version:** If the version in `composer.lock` does not match the test shop (e.g. 6.6 against 6.7), the setup stops with the matching image (e.g. `dockware/shopware:6.6.10.4`), which you can set in the sandbox environment – or **Continue anyway**.
- **Working with the AI:** The AI knows the project’s layout and which plugin brings what. It changes an existing plugin in its folder, creates a new plugin under `custom/plugins/<Name>` and configuration in `config/packages/`. Every change reaches the test shop at once; if it changes a configuration file, Lotse checks it again.
- **Submit for review and ZIP:** Only changes to the project’s own parts are published (`custom/`, `config/`, `src/`, `tests/`, `composer.json`/`composer.lock` …) – never `vendor/`, `var/`, `public/`, `.env` or workflows. **Plugin as ZIP** asks which plugin of the project you want to download.
- **Limits of “Overlay”:** Packages the project requires that the test shop lacks are named in the log; whatever depends on them does not run there – installed completely, `composer install` fetches them. In both modes: files in `config/packages/prod/` and `when@prod` have no effect, because the test shop runs in development mode.

### Start sandbox: every case

The “Start sandbox” dialog has three short steps; each has a sensible default, “Next” only works once the step is complete, and the steps at the top can be clicked to go back. It fits a phone, too.

1. **What?** – **Existing project** (default: plugin, theme or whole shop from GitHub; Lotse detects what it is), **New plugin** (the AI builds one in an empty or newly created GitHub project), **Start without a project** (just a test shop) or **Own setup** (you say where the code goes and what runs during the setup). Plus an optional title.
2. **Code source** – project and starting point. Lotse looks at the project **before** the start and shows the result as a hint, e.g. “Detected: plugin AcmeTheme”, “Detected: whole Shopware shop (3 plugins)”, “empty project → new plugin” or “Not recognised → own setup”. For this it briefly clones the project into a temporary folder (this takes a while for large projects); if that fails, the dialog says so and you can still start – the setup then detects it itself. **change** switches between “as detected” and “Own setup”. If the project brings a `.lotse/sandbox.json` with commands, the dialog shows them here for approval (see below). **Advanced** (folded; open for an own setup) holds the **target folder in the container**, **own commands** (at most 10) and **variables** (at most 20, not encrypted – secrets belong in the environment; they take precedence over environment variables of the same name) for this sandbox only, plus another **image** tag (e.g. `6.7.14.0`, pinned versions of the configured image only), the **preview start page** and **notes for the AI (this sandbox only)**. Image and start page take precedence over `.lotse/sandbox.json`. With **Start without a project** the step only asks for the plugin’s technical name.
3. **Data** – **Demo data** (default, fastest), a **stored database** (the environment’s default is preselected) or **Upload a dump**.

**Own setup.** For anything that is not a Shopware plugin or project (or when you want to be in control): Lotse clones the repository, puts it into the target folder in the container and only runs your commands – it installs, activates and compiles nothing and assumes nothing about Shopware. The target must be an absolute folder below `/var/www`, `/opt` or `/srv` (e.g. `/opt/app` or `/var/www/html`); the default is `/opt/<project name>`. The sandbox’s own commands and those from `.lotse/sandbox.json` run in that folder (the environment’s commands still in `/var/www/html`), and so do the AI’s commands. The container is still a Dockware Shopware; your commands decide what of it you use. Lotse only overwrites files from the repository in the target folder and, on the next full sync, only removes what it put there itself. The setup is in your hands here: if a command fails, its output is in the “Setup” tab.

**Start without a project.** A test shop with demo data and no GitHub at all: the working copy is a local git (its base is an empty start commit), the AI creates a new plugin in it (technical name suggested from the title) and saves its steps as usual. The **Review** tab then offers **Create as GitHub project**: Lotse creates a private repository (account and name of your choice), uploads the starting point as `main`, and from then on you submit the changes for review like with any project. If the upload fails (e.g. the token lacks write access), the empty repository stays; a second click with the same name uses it again. As long as there is no GitHub project, the AI’s commits and changes exist only in the sandbox – Lotse then refuses “Retry from here” from “Clone repository” (or earlier), because it would delete them. Downloading the plugin as a ZIP works without GitHub, too.

**Settings in the project: `.lotse/sandbox.json`.** A repository can say itself how its sandbox is set up – similar to a devcontainer file:

```json
{
  "mode": "custom",
  "targetPath": "/opt/app",
  "commands": [
    { "label": "Dependencies", "command": "composer install --no-interaction", "phase": "after-db" },
    { "label": "Demo data", "command": "/var/www/html/bin/console framework:demodata", "phase": "final" }
  ],
  "env": ["STRIPE_KEY"],
  "image": "6.7.14.0",
  "previewPath": "/en/",
  "notes": "Test customer: test@example.com. The theme is AcmeTheme."
}
```

- `mode` – `plugin`, `project` or `custom` (own setup); `targetPath` only with `custom`. `projectDir` – the repository folder that holds the Shopware project (e.g. `"shop"` or `"apps/shop"`, at most three levels); needed only when several projects are in the repository, otherwise Lotse finds the folder itself. If `plugin` or `project` does not match what Lotse detects, the setup stops at “Detect plugin” with a hint. `projectInstall` – `full` (default: the whole repository, `composer install` after you allowed internet) or `overlay` (only plugins, apps, configuration and `src/` over the test shop’s Shopware, without internet); only for a whole Shopware project.
- `commands` – setup commands with `label`, `command`, `phase` (`after-db` or `final`, default `final`) and optionally `timeoutSec` (10–3600). With `custom` they run in `targetPath` (so Shopware’s `bin/console` needs its full path `/var/www/html/bin/console`), otherwise in `/var/www/html`. **They never run without you:** the dialog shows them before the start (“This project wants to run the following commands during the setup …”) with **Allow commands** and **Without these commands**. Lotse remembers the approval for this repository and exactly this content of the file; if the file changes (also through the AI in the working copy), Lotse asks again – the setup then stops at “Detect plugin” and shows the same card. Like all own commands they only run inside the container.
- `env` – only the **names** of environment variables the project needs; the values always come from the environment in Lotse. A missing one is named by the dialog and the log.
- `image` – another tag of the configured image (e.g. `6.7.14.0`), only with a fixed version and only from where the environment’s image comes from.
- `previewPath` – the page the preview starts with.
- `notes` – notes for the AI; it reads them as information from the repository, not as instructions.

Unknown settings are ignored (the log names them). If the file is invalid, the dialog says which setting – the setup would stop at “Detect plugin” with the same message.

**Notes for the AI.** The environment (“Sandbox Coding” tab) has a free-text field for your own knowledge about setup and project (at most 4000 characters). Every sandbox’s AI gets it as information, not as an instruction – it changes nothing about its rules (no live shop, no secrets). The notes under “Advanced” in the start dialog are added for that one sandbox.

### Saved environments: set up once, a fresh sandbox for every job

The most convenient way to work: connect the project, upload the database and set everything up **once** – then **save it as an environment**. For every new job (build a plugin, adjust the theme, SEO texts in the template …) you start a **fresh sandbox** from it, in a chat of its own. If a job needs nothing in particular, take the **demo shop without a project**.

Everything the start dialog asks is saved – case, project and branch (or “without a project” with the plugin name), target folder, own commands and variables, image, preview start page, notes for the AI and the data (demo data or a stored database). At most 50.

**The same choices everywhere**, top to bottom: your saved environments (the **default** first), **Demo shop without a project** (Shopware with demo data, no repository, one click; changes stay in the local git) and **Set up new …** (the dialog in three steps). This list opens from:

- **Start sandbox** below the workspace’s input – for the current chat; below it **Manage saved environments** leads to the list.
- **New sandbox** in the **Sandbox Coding** tab – the sandbox starts in a new chat.
- the **Proposal: start a sandbox** card when the AI proposes a sandbox: it knows the names and contents of your saved environments (without secret values) and may preselect one (“The AI suggests the saved environment …”). You confirm with **Start sandbox** or pick something else; for **Set up new …** the button reads the same and opens the dialog with the project the AI proposed. Nothing starts before your click; the AI’s reason becomes the sandbox’s title.

Each entry shows in a second line what it uses (e.g. “Existing project · acme/shop @ main · live.sql · Image 6.7.14.0”). A click on an environment starts right away, without the dialog; the status line confirms it (“New sandbox from “Shop base” started: …”).

- **Every start is a new sandbox.** Its own container, its own copy of the database (from the stored database’s snapshot once there is one), its own working folder and its own branch `lotse/<title>-<id>`. Several jobs from the same environment run side by side (as many as `LOTSE_SANDBOX_MAX` allows at once; for one more, Lotse pauses the least recently used and says so in the new one’s chat, e.g. ““Build plugin” was paused because at most 2 sandboxes run at the same time.”). They share nothing: no files, no database, no preview cart session, no consoles, no internet approval. What a sandbox changes does not change the saved environment.
- **Save:** in the start dialog’s last step **Save as environment** (name, optionally “Make default”), or for a running sandbox in the “⋯” menu **Save setup as environment** – it keeps how the sandbox was started, not its changes. A dump uploaded only once cannot be kept; tick “Store for further sandboxes” when uploading. At the top of the start dialog a saved environment can be picked and then changed; the default one is preselected there.
- **Checked on every start:** Lotse first checks that the project is still reachable and has the branch, that the stored database still exists and that the image is allowed. If something does not fit, nothing starts: the start dialog opens with all of the environment’s choices and says at the top what to change. If the stored database was deleted, **Data** deliberately has nothing preselected (not the demo data either) – you decide what the sandbox starts with.
- **Secrets stay in the sandbox environment:** a saved environment never holds values of secret environment variables – such a variable (or a value containing a secret) is kept by name only; the dialog shows such variables under “Advanced” as “From the sandbox environment” and says after saving which ones are affected. If one is missing later, Lotse says so at the start. If a secret value is in another field (command, notes for the AI, start page …), Lotse saves nothing and names the field – use the name there instead, in commands e.g. `$STRIPE_KEY`.
- **Approvals stay approvals:** commands from `.lotse/sandbox.json` also run from a saved environment only after your approval of exactly that file content; the setup asks as usual.
- **Manage:** in the **Sandbox Coding** tab under **Saved environments**: edit (in the same dialog), rename, duplicate, mark as default and delete; plus “last started”. Creating, changing, deleting and every start are in the audit log (“Gespeicherte Umgebung: …”, with the sandbox started).

Not to be confused with the **sandbox environment** (tab **Sandbox Coding**, collapsible at the bottom): it applies to all sandboxes – image, presets, internet, variables with secret values, own commands and notes for the AI.

### Without a developer: build your own plugin

You run a Shopware shop but have no developer? With a sandbox the AI builds a small plugin for you – a shipping note, a banner, an extra field –, you check it in a test shop and then upload it to your shop yourself. The live shop stays untouched until you install the plugin yourself.

1. **Prepare once:** switch on **Sandbox Coding** in the settings (your administrator sets up Docker for it, see [Self-hosting](self-hosting.md#enable-sandbox-coding)) and connect **GitHub** under Integrations. For Lotse to create a new project for you, the token needs the permission **Administration: Read and write** and repository access **All repositories** in addition to **Contents** and **Pull requests** (classic token: scope `repo`). Without it, simply create the repository on GitHub yourself (private, with a README) and pick it in the dialog. It also works without GitHub at all: **Start without a project** (see [Start sandbox: every case](#start-sandbox-every-case)) – the plugin then goes into the shop as a ZIP.
2. **Create a project:** in a new chat click **Start sandbox**, choose **New plugin** under **What?**, then **Create a new project** in the **Code source** step: choose the account (personal or an organisation), enter a name (e.g. `shipping-note-plugin`) and click **Create project**. Lotse creates a **private** repository with a README on GitHub and selects it. If the token lacks the permission, the dialog says exactly which one. Then the dialog asks for the plugin’s **technical name** (suggested from the project name, ideally with your vendor prefix first, e.g. `MiaShippingNote`); under **Data** the demo data will do – then **Start sandbox**.
3. **Describe what you want:** while the chat is empty, examples are shown below the input (“Show a slim banner above the header …”, “Add an optional … field to the checkout”, “Build my own plugin that shows a shipping note on the product page …”); a click copies the text for you to adjust. Write in your own words what customers should see and where.
4. **Check in the test shop:** the AI builds the plugin, installs it in the test shop and tells you in plain words what changed and which page to open in the **Preview**. If you don’t like something, write it in the chat or mark it with **Annotate** right in the preview. The AI saves each finished step (**Changes** tab → “Saved steps”).
5. **Get it into the shop – two ways:**
   - **Download plugin as ZIP** (**Review** tab, or **ZIP** in the list on the **Sandbox Coding** tab): Lotse checks `composer.json` first and tells you what is missing – exactly what Shopware needs, otherwise it silently skips an uploaded plugin (e.g. description, version or display name) – **Ask the AI to add this** sends the list to the AI. Once everything is there, the button downloads `<Name>-<version>.zip`. In the Shopware admin: **Extensions → My extensions → Upload extension**, then install and activate it. Ideally try the plugin in a test environment of your shop first.
   - **Submit for review**: opens a pull request on GitHub that e.g. an agency reviews and takes over.
6. **Finish:** once the plugin is in the shop, **Finish sandbox** (menu **⋯**). The code stays in the project on GitHub; for later changes just start a new sandbox on the same project.

The ZIP contains the plugin as it is in the sandbox right now (including changes not submitted yet), in a folder named after the technical name – without `.git`, `node_modules`, tests, CI and editor folders, `.env*`, keys and other secret files. `vendor/` is only included when `composer.json` requires packages besides Shopware and the folder exists. If a file contains a key, token or credentials (also a password stored in Lotse or an address with user and password), there is no ZIP. Every download is in the audit log. Lotse never installs anything into the live shop directly. You can also ask the AI to get the plugin ready “as a ZIP for the live shop”: it completes `composer.json`, saves everything and shows a **Plugin as a ZIP for the live shop** card in the chat. The button only opens the **Review** tab; the ZIP is checked and downloaded only when you click there.

### Language
The interface and AI replies are available in **German** and **English**. You can switch in **Settings** and at the bottom of the side navigation (DE/EN). Without an explicit choice, Lotse follows your browser language (otherwise German); your choice is stored in the `lotse_lang` cookie and takes effect immediately.

- The AI's **chat replies** (answers, follow-up questions, summaries, reasons in proposals) follow the interface language.
- **Shop content** (product copy, pages, SEO, translations) is written by Lotse in the language the shop or sales channel requires, not automatically in the interface language.
- Text that comes directly from the shop or the server (shop data, some error messages) stays in its original language.

## 10. What Lotse does not do

- It never writes without approval and does not read customer, order or payment data.
- It does not estimate prices, stock levels or purchase prices. You decide these values.
- It does not give legal advice; legal and accessibility checks are technical hints.
- It does not replace staging: try new workflows in a test shop first.

## 11. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | What to do |
|---|---|
| “Host nicht erlaubt” (host not allowed) | You are accessing Lotse under a name that is not allowed. When running on a server, add the name to `ALLOWED_HOSTS` ([Self-hosting](self-hosting.md)) |
| Chat keeps showing “running” | Reload the page; Lotse reconnects. A dot in the history shows the real state |
| Preview shows “Redirect outside the shop” | The shop redirects to a foreign domain. The same site (e.g. `stage.` → `www.`) is followed, foreign domains are not |
| Shop check “incomplete” | At least half of the checks failed (shop unreachable, access blocked). Test the connection, then “Check again” |
| New check reports nothing | The rule currently matches no active product; the match count before saving shows this |
| Sandbox setup stops at “Detect plugin” (or “Detect Shopware project”), status “Waiting for your approval” | The project brings commands in `.lotse/sandbox.json` that are not approved yet, or the file has changed: in the **Setup** tab, **Allow commands** or **Without these commands**. If the file is invalid, the message names the setting – fix it in the project and retry from “Detect plugin” |
